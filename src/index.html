ANCIENT ROME

REPUBLIC

509 BC - 27 BC

The Roman Republic began with the legendary founding of the city by Romulus, followed by the overthrow of King Tarquinius Superbus. During this era, Rome was ruled by a pair of consuls and the patrician Senate

PRINCIPATE

27 BC - 284 AD

After Gaius Julius Caesar instated himself as dictator for life and was subsequently assassinated, his grand-nephew Octavian became the first princeps, Augustus, and began the era of the Roman Empire known as the Principate.

DOMINATE

284 AD - 476 AD

The emperor Diocletian adopted the title dominus, thus beginning the Dominate era of the Roman Empire. In 395 AD, the Roman tetrarchy split into the Eastern and Western Roman Empire. The Western Empire then fell in 476 AD.

THE IMPERIAL PRINCIPATE


In 44 BC, the famed Roman general Gaius Julius Caesar assumed the role of dictator perpetuo (dictator for life). He was assassinated on the Ides of March only a few weeks later. This led to a civil war between the Second Triumvirate (Julius Caesar's grand-nephew Octavian, Mark Antony, and Marcus Aemillius Lepidus) against the conspirators. After defeating the assassins, however, Octavian and Antony became bitter rivals. Octavian subsequently defeated Antony and Cleopatra at the Battle of Actium in 31 BC. Octavian, now called Augustus Caesar, carefully developed the role of princeps (principal citizen) to pacify the Senate by acting like he did not desire a monarchy. However, he gained significant political power and began a new era of administrative and legal reformation. Augustus' rule was also the start of Pax Romana (Roman Peace), which lasted until the death of Marcus Aurelius in 180 AD.
